Experiencing Evening Colors at CFAY: A Timeless Navy Tradition in Yokosuka

📍 Fleet Activities Yokosuka Main Gate

Watch on YouTube →

There is a quiet magic that settles over Commander, Fleet Activities Yokosuka (CFAY) as dusk begins to fall. If you ever find yourself wandering near the headquarters building of Commander, U.S. Naval Forces Japan and Commander Navy Region Japan around sunset, everything suddenly slows down.

As the sky fills with soft evening hues, a long-standing military tradition unfolds: Evening Colors.


For those unfamiliar, Evening Colors is the ceremonial lowering of the national ensign at sunset. In Yokosuka, this tradition holds a unique charm, blending core U.S. Navy customs with the serene backdrop of the Japanese hills. As the bugle call plays across the base, everything comes to a standstill—vehicles stop, pedestrians face the flag, and a respectful hush falls over the area.


In front of the headquarters building, detail-assigned sailors in sharp whites meticulously work to lower, fold, and secure the American flag, the Japanese ensign, and the surrounding flags. Watching the team coordinate each motion against the evening sky is a striking reminder of the shared history, discipline, and daily rhythm that defines life on base in Japan.


It’s easy to get caught up in the hustle of living abroad and exploring new sights, but taking a minute to stand quietly and watch Evening Colors is one of those subtle moments that sticks with you. Whether you’re stationed here, visiting, or simply following along on our adventures, it’s a powerful touchstone of life overseas with the Navy.
